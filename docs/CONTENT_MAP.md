# Content Map — The Mystery of Fire

**Lesson:** The Role of Air in Burning Things  
**Grade:** 6 Science  
**Central Question:** What does a flame need to keep burning?  
**Source Authority:** `assets/source/lesson-page-1.jpeg`, `lesson-page-2.jpeg`, `lesson-page-3.jpeg`

---

## Extracted Content

### Definition (Authoritative)
**Burning (Combustion):** A reaction (combination) that occurs between oxygen and a substance that produces heat and light.

### Opening Context
- Atmospheric air is all around us.
- Air is invisible to the eye.
- Air plays a crucial role in many processes, particularly burning (combustion).

### Experiment Materials
| Item | Role |
|------|------|
| Wooden board | Base platform |
| Clay | Fixes candle; can create bottom gaps / seals |
| Candle | Fuel source (burning substance) |
| Bottomless glass jar | Enclosure / chimney |
| Metal lid | Seals top opening |
| Lighter | Lights candle — **teacher only** |

### Safety
> Caution: Only the teacher should use the lighter to light the candle.

### Procedure & Observations

| Step | Action | Observation (Authoritative) |
|------|--------|------------------------------|
| 1 | Spread clay on wooden board, fix candle, teacher lights it | Flame burns (setup) |
| 2 | Cover with bottomless glass jar; leave top open | Flame continues (implied by sequence) |
| 3 | Close jar with metal lid | Flame gradually weakens; candle goes out after a short time |
| 4 | Repeat with a small gap at the bottom of the jar in the clay (lid on) | Candle flame goes out after a slightly longer time |
| 5 | Take off metal lid: gap at bottom **and** gap at top | Candle flame continues to burn |

### Scientific Explanation (Authoritative)
1. **Closed jar:** The air inside the jar is used up, and once it runs out the candle goes out.
2. **Open jar with bottom opening and top opening:** The air is constantly renewed (replaced) from the outside surroundings, allowing the candle to continue burning.

### Conclusions (Authoritative — Do Not Alter Meaning)
1. Air has a role in burning.
2. One of the most important conditions for things to continue burning is that **fresh air** must constantly flow in and out.
3. In a place where air is not renewed, things cannot continue burning.

## Vocabulary
| Term | Student-facing meaning |
|------|------------------------|
| Burning / Combustion | Oxygen + substance → heat + light |
| Air | Invisible gas mixture around us; contains oxygen |
| Oxygen | Part of air needed for burning |
| Fresh air | New air that replaces used air |
| Renewed / Replaced | Continuous supply of new air |
| Closed jar | Sealed so air cannot flow in or out |
| Gap / Opening | Path for air to enter or leave |

---

## What Must NOT Be Invented
- Do not claim fire needs only oxygen without mentioning the substance (fuel) and heat/light product from the source definition.
- Do not claim a bottom gap alone is enough for continued burning (Step 4 proves it still goes out).
- Do not invent quantitative oxygen percentages beyond Grade 6 framing unless clearly labeled as optional enrichment.
- Do not contradict: both top and bottom openings are needed for continuous renewal in this experiment.
- Teacher-only lighter rule must remain.
